03 — OriginsRewinding · 2018 – 2022

The backstory.

Between 13 and 16, I built and ran a full creative and commercial operation inside Roblox — 3D environments, clothing design, multi-client advertising, commission management. I didn't know what any of it was called. I let my curiosity lead me.

Subway build · age 15
GLXW weekly designs · age 15
AGE 11

First build.

Started building 3D environments in Roblox Studio. Coded very basic Lua scripts to make things actually work, learned to make the most out of what already existed.

AGE 13

Environments. Design. Learning.

The outcomes of lockdown. Continued to learn, iterate, and test. Built a Paris-themed shopping district with multiple storefronts and interior environments in Roblox Studio. Started designing 2D clothing in Paint.NET — first attempts, figuring out layers, shading, templates. Also started teaching myself Blender for small personal projects: basic models, simple GFX, just exploring what the tool could do.

The clothing work continued through age 14. The improvement was visible.

AGE 15

Hired. Commissioned. Documented.

The clothing work had levelled up enough to get hired. GLXW brought me on as a weekly designer — fixed rate per piece, paid by output. I was also taking ad commissions on the side: designing advertising banners for other groups, each with a different aesthetic and brief.

Built a commission queue system so clients could see their position, status, and updates all in one place. Not because the process was broken — I just thought that was the right way to treat people waiting on work.

I took advantage of my high school's Adobe suite and documented the whole design process on YouTube. I learned how to use Photoshop, Illustrator, and Premiere Pro. I posted speed recordings of clothing from start to finish and amassed thousands of views. Comments still come in, most recent was three months ago.

Running it all simultaneously with school and two part-time jobs at one point. Continued to build on Roblox Studio from time to time.

04 — PresentBack to 2026

Back to the present.

Somewhere along the way, I stopped thinking about any of this as "experience."

High school happened. Engineering happened. Life got busier. What once filled most of my free time slowly faded into something I barely thought about.

But looking back, those years shaped me more than I ever realised.

Without knowing the words for any of it, I'd spent years learning how to build things from nothing — teaching myself unfamiliar tools, working inside constraints, taking feedback, managing people who were waiting on me, and caring about details long after anyone would have noticed. A design that finally looked right. An environment that felt real. A stranger who felt looked after while their commission sat in the queue.

The strange part is how those same instincts keep resurfacing.

Today I study Mechatronics Engineering at the University of Canterbury, pulled toward the same things I always was — how systems behave, how problems come apart, where the technical meets the human. And outside the degree, I keep drifting toward the rooms where those worlds overlap: engineering communities, startups, late innovation nights, the people quietly building something.

For a long time I thought I was just making things on the internet.

Turns out I was learning how I think — and I've been thinking that way ever since.
